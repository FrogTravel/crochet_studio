01

Materials & Gauge

What you'll need before you begin

This blouse is worked in fine cotton in multiple separate panels, then assembled. The original Japanese pattern uses a lightweight mercerized cotton at 2mm hook. A natural ecru or off-white cotton gives the best lace definition.

Yarn

Fine mercerized cotton (approx. 4-ply / fingering weight). Catania col. 00105 (Natur/ecru) works beautifully. You'll need approximately 400–500g depending on size. Buy all skeins from the same dye lot.

Hook

2.0 mm steel crochet hook (or 2.5mm if your tension runs tight). The pattern uses a fine hook to give crisp, open lacework. Check your gauge before starting the main body.

Notions

5–6 small pearl or shell buttons (approx. 8–10mm) for back closure. Stitch markers. Tapestry needle for seaming. Blocking mat and rust-proof pins.

Gauge

Work a 10 cm × 10 cm swatch in fan stitch. Each fan unit = approx. 1.5–2 cm wide. Adjust hook size until your gauge matches. Gauge is critical — even 1mm difference per fan will alter the fit significantly across 30+ repeats.

Skill Level This is an intermediate-to-advanced project. The fan stitch itself is straightforward, but multi-piece construction and shaping require careful attention. Comfortable with dc, sc, and basic shaping? You're ready.
02

Stitch Guide

Three patterns used throughout the blouse

Pattern A — Fan / Shell Stitch (Main Body)

This is the dominant stitch used for all body panels. Each fan consists of 5 double crochets fanning into a single stitch, creating the distinctive downward triangle texture visible throughout the garment.

Pattern A — Fan Stitch (5-dc fan) · Rows alternate position like brickwork

Foundation: Chain multiple of 6 + 3
Row 1: 1 sc in 2nd ch from hook, *skip 2 ch, 5 dc into next ch (fan), skip 2 ch, 1 sc; rep * to end
Row 2: Ch 3, turn. 2 dc in first sc, *sc in center dc of fan, 5 dc in next sc; rep *, end 3 dc in last sc
Row 3: Ch 1, turn. Sc in first dc, *5 dc in next sc, sc in center of fan; rep * to end
Repeat: Rows 2–3 for pattern

Pattern B — Scallop Edging (縁編みB)

Used on all finished edges: hem, sleeve ruffle outer edge, neckline, and the waist braid. Creates the delicate wave visible along the bottom of the blouse in the finished photo.

Pattern B — Scallop Edging with picot tops

Pattern C — Ruffle Lace (模様編みC)

Used exclusively for the flutter sleeve ruffle panels. A fan-on-fan construction creates a wider outer edge than inner edge, giving the ruffle its natural flare. Work back and forth — do not join in the round.

Japanese Symbol Key ○— = attach new yarn  |  ▶ = cut yarn  |  ↩ = carry / pass yarn to next section

Construction Overview

10 separate pieces, then assembled

Unlike many garments crocheted in the round, this blouse is constructed flat in panels that are seamed together. This gives precise shaping and allows the lace pattern to always read correctly.

04

Step-by-Step Instructions

Work each piece individually, then assemble

1

Front Center Panel (5図)

This is your starting piece and the foundation of the blouse. Chain enough stitches for your base width — approximately 63–65 fan repeats for a medium size (roughly 375–390 chains for a 6-chain repeat). The bottom of this piece is your hem width.

Work upward in fan stitch (Pattern A), maintaining full fans across. As you approach the bust, begin very gradual waist shaping by working the edge fans as half-fans if needed (work only 3 dc instead of 5 at the side edges on a few rows).

For the neckline: when you have approximately 20–21 rows worked, the piece divides. Work each shoulder separately. On the right side, decrease 1 fan per row at the neckline edge for 4–5 rows until you have a strap width of approximately 4–5 fans. Fasten off, then join yarn to left side and mirror.

The neckline is a wide, shallow scoop — shallower at front center and deeper toward the sides. Work 1 round of sc (細編み) around the entire neckline edge to stabilize it.

2

Back Panels (1図, 3図, 8図, 9図)

The back right (1図) is approximately the same height as the front but narrows to a higher neckline. Work approximately 29–30 rows total. The back neckline is a gentle curve rather than the deep front scoop.

Button band: Along the center-back edge of the right back piece, work 1 row of sc evenly down the full length. Then work a second row of sc, adding button loops: at evenly spaced intervals (approximately every 2.5–3 cm), ch 4, skip 2 sc, sc — this creates the button loop. You'll need 5–6 loops total. For the left back, work 2 rows of plain sc — no loops.

The back side panels (3図, 8図, 9図) are narrower fill pieces that connect the back to the side seams. Work them to the same row height as the adjacent main pieces and keep stitch count consistent for a smooth seam.

3

Front Side Panels (6図, 7図, 10図)

The right (6図) and left (7図) front side panels are mirror images of each other. They are wider at the bottom and shoulder than at the waist — this creates the fitted waist silhouette.

Crucially, the shoulder area widens significantly: these panels form the base for the flutter ruffle sleeves. Work about 14–15 rows, increasing the stitch count at the top 3–4 rows to create a broad shoulder shelf (approximately 8–10 fans wide at the top vs. 5–6 at the waist).

The small front side infill pieces (10図) are short, rectangular panels worked in Pattern A and Pattern B edging to fill the underarm area between the side panels and back.

4

Waist Braid / Band (ブレード)

Chain 121 stitches (155 for the larger size). The band is approximately 1.1–1.5 cm tall (small/medium).

Work 2 rows of Pattern B scallop edging on both long edges of the chain. The short ends are approximately 33.5 cm / 31 rows for the smaller size. This creates a firm, decorative band that cinches the waist and visually separates the body from the hem.

Tip Block the waist band before attaching it. This helps the scallops sit flat and even, and makes it much easier to join to the body with consistent tension.
5

Flutter Ruffle Panels (フリル)

Work two ruffle panels (one per side) in Pattern C. Chain approximately 63 stitches for your foundation. The ruffle is wider at the outer hem edge than the inner armhole edge — this flare is built into Pattern C naturally as you fan out into the outer rows.

Work approximately 11 rows total. The inner straight edge will attach to the armhole; the curved outer edge receives Pattern B scallop edging. The ruffle frills in Pattern C create the overlapping shell-lace visible in the finished photo.

Block the ruffles aggressively — pin them flat in a gentle arc and allow to dry completely before attaching.

05

Assembly

Seaming order matters — follow this sequence

Block all pieces before assembly. Wet-block in cool water, gently squeeze (do not wring), pin to measurements, and allow to dry completely. The lace will open up beautifully and pieces will align more easily.

  • Join all side panels to front center and back pieces using flat seam. Work from hem up to underarm, then continue up the side to the armhole opening.
  • Join shoulder seams — connect right front strap to right back shoulder, and left front strap to left back shoulder. These are short seams but critical for fit.
  • Attach the waist braid with a slip stitch join along the lower body, centering the braid at the narrowest waist point. The braid should overlap slightly onto both the main body and the hem section.
  • Attach the ruffle panels to the armhole openings, sc joining the straight inner edge of each ruffle to the armhole edge. Ease the ruffle gently — do not pull tight.
  • Sew pearl buttons to the left back band, aligning precisely with the button loops on the right back. Space evenly over the full back opening length.
  • Final block the completed garment. Lay flat, pin to finished measurements, and allow to dry fully.
06

Tips & Notes

For best results

Dye Lots

Buy all yarn at once from the same dye lot. With 400–500g needed, even a subtle lot difference will show in the finished fabric — especially in natural ecru where variation is most visible.

Tension Consistency

Take breaks and check your tension regularly. Fatigue causes tension to tighten. If working across multiple sessions, always start the new session with a few practice stitches to re-establish your gauge.

Blocking is Essential

This lace will look unimpressive before blocking. Wet blocking with pinning opens the stitch pattern dramatically and is the difference between "homemade" and "heirloom."

Sizing

The pattern includes at least 3 sizes. Numbers in parentheses on the diagrams correspond to larger sizes. When sizing up, use the larger stitch counts consistently across all pieces.
